Design case · ADDIE · ISO 13485 context

Rehearse the hands before they touch the device.

A MedTech sewing station attaches a fabric sewing cuff to the stent of a Class III implantable heart valve — a manual suturing operation where a mis-placed stitch, uneven tension or a skipped hole becomes a scrapped unit or, worse, a patient-safety field complaint. New operators learn on real material, so every error destroys a controlled component and their first weeks carry the highest defect rate.

This case designs a 4D simulator: a digital twin of the piece the operator can rotate, pull apart, handle and suture — in time — before cutting a single real thread. Learning by doing, with zero scrap while learning.

Regulatory frame — worst-case by design: the part is treated as a Class III implantable prosthesis (FDA) — a heart-valve sewing cuff, life-sustaining, PMA pathway — so a suturing defect is a patient-safety event, not merely scrap. Work runs under an ISO 13485 QMS with 21 CFR 820 (QMSR) and EU MDR controls, inside a cleanroom (ISO 14644) under GMP / GxP, and every unit carries a Device History Record (DHR). This maximises the zero-error bar and brings cleanroom readiness into scope.

The signature · 4D learning-by-doing flow

Disassemble → Understand → Handle → Suture (in time).

The "4D" is 3D geometry plus time: the operator doesn't just see the piece — they move through the procedure step by step. Four stages, each a doing-task, not a watching-task:

  1. Disassemble — explode the digital twin into cuff / stent / core and name each part.
  2. Understand — rotate it to see the seam and every suture hole from any angle.
  3. Handle — reseat the parts; feel the orientation that must match the real jig.
  4. Suture in time — advance the procedure hole by hole; the sim coaches angle, tension and sequence, and blocks a skipped hole.

      D6 · Assessment & Kirkpatrick plan

      The Kirkpatrick × zero-error model.

      Safety/compliance-critical steps get a zero-error gate: the learner cannot be marked complete until a full run has no sequence errors and tension within tolerance. Applied to exactly the critical steps — not sprinkled everywhere (over-gating frustrates and teaches nothing).

      LevelWhat we measureInstrument
      L1Reaction — clarity & psychological safety (blame-free practice).2-item confidence + usability prompt in-course
      L2Learning — total mastery: suture to spec, unaided.Zero-error mastery run (gated) + label-the-part check
      L3Behaviour — supervised performance on real units.Supervisor sign-off on first 5 live units; xAPI from the bench
      L4Results — absence of incidents & audit findings; yield.First-pass yield, scrap/operator & deviations vs. baseline
      The honest line: the simulator proves L1–L2; L3–L4 need the plant's quality data, so the design hooks them (xAPI, a defined baseline) rather than claiming them. For a Class III device the L2 zero-error gate is the prerequisite to DHR sign-off — no mastery, no live unit. Leading indicators — time-to-competency and retraining rate — are tracked against quality-system deviations.
      D7 · SCORM / cmi5 / xAPI packaging

      What the LMS records, and why cmi5.

      For a controlled training record, completion and success are separated and the mastery logic reaches the LMS cleanly:

      • completion = all four stages visited; success = zero-error mastery run passed. Two different flags — an operator can finish the lesson without yet passing the gate.
      • cmi5 (xAPI) over SCORM 1.2: each statement is actor · verb · object · result (e.g. passed the gate), streamed to an LRS and reportable through an enterprise LMS such as Cornerstone — and it lets us add on-the-job (L3) statements from the bench to the same LRS.
      • Reported to LMS/LRS: mastery pass, sequence-error count, tension variance, attempts-to-mastery, time-in-sim. These become the training record and the early-warning signal for a struggling operator.
      Traceability closes here: every screen → an objective (D3) → a task (D2) → an assessment item (D6) → an LMS/LRS record (D7). No orphans. That chain is what makes the course auditable under ISO 13485 / 21 CFR 820.
