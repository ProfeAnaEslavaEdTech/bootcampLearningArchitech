Core Concepts

Facilitation vs. Presentation: the decisive difference

A presentation moves information from the speaker to the audience. A facilitated session moves the audience through an experience that changes what they can do. The difference is not confidence or delivery style — it is design.

The RSVP scaffold (four required segments)

0:00–5:00
R — Relate Activate prior knowledge
Poll, question, or provocative statement. The learner connects this session to something they already know or care about. Never start with logistics.
5:00–18:00
S — Show The core concept (10–13 min max)
One idea, explained clearly. If you need more, break it into two segments with an Apply between them. Rule: no more than 10 consecutive minutes of exposition.
18:00–25:00
V — Verify Participants apply the concept
Chat storm, breakout room, quick case, annotate a slide. The facilitator falls silent. Learning happens here, not during the exposition.
25:00–30:00
P — Pull Forward Transfer bridge + call to action
What will they do differently on Monday? Name it. Give one resource. The session ends with a decision, not a summary slide.
Bloom's level: RSVP targets B3 Apply (Verify segment) over B1 Remember and B2 Understand (Show segment). A session that only covers B1–B2 is a lecture, not facilitated learning — and it will show in a recording.

Engagement techniques for virtual delivery

  • Chat storm: ask a question, give 60 seconds for everyone to type (not send yet), then say "send now." Creates simultaneous visible participation.
  • Annotation: share a diagram and ask participants to circle the most surprising element. Works in Zoom Annotate and Teams Whiteboard.
  • Breakout rooms (2-person, 4 min): the smallest possible group eliminates social loafing. Use for the Verify segment.
  • Warm/cold call with choice: "Who wants to share?" then 3-second pause, then name someone specifically — but offer an exit ("or you can pass"). Maintains energy without humiliating.
  • Running chat transcript as a whiteboard: paste key points into the chat as you teach; participants see, can scroll back, and reference it after.
Avoid: rhetorical questions ("doesn't that make sense?"), reading slides aloud, turning off camera to share screen, 15-minute Zoom polls with no debrief. Each of these signals you are presenting, not facilitating.

Module 1 Output

Session Guide — the artefact for Module 2

Before you open your screen-recording software, you need one document. This is what Module 2 will build from.

Session Guide — template

  • Title & audience — one sentence: who is this for and what will they be able to do?
  • TLO (Mager ABCD) — given [condition], [observable verb] [task] [criterion]
  • Bloom level — B3 Apply minimum; explain why
  • RSVP sequence — four segments with timecodes and what happens in each
  • Engagement technique for Verify segment — name the technique and the exact prompt you will give participants
  • Transfer bridge — the one sentence that ends the session
  • Slide count estimate — target 8–12 slides maximum
  • Tech setup — Zoom, Teams, or OBS; camera on; microphone check
Next step — Module 2: You will take this Session Guide and build the slides using the Ocean Ink design system, then record using OBS or Zoom's built-in recorder. Module 2 covers the production checklist and the recording workflow.
